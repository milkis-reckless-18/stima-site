5Research

5.1 What we research and why

We study how people work with AI on real tasks: which prompts people write, when they accept model output and when they reject it, what they verify, and how that relates to the quality of what they produce. We publish the results in academic venues.

The purpose is methodological. We are trying to establish how assessments of human-AI collaboration should be designed and scored, which is a question the field has not settled and which affects everyone assessed by a tool like ours. 5.2 Two kinds of research, and only one of them is optional

Aggregate Research. Not optional in the hiring context, and we tell you about it before the assessment. In the education context it does not happen at all unless the institution has signed a research agreement.

We strip identifiers out of session data and use what is left to improve our scoring and to publish statistics and aggregate findings. Nobody is named. No individual session is published this way, whether or not identifiers have been removed. This is described on the assessment notice screen, before the optional question, so that the optional question is not the first you hear of it.

Stripping is not a single pass. We remove direct identifiers automatically, then run automated detection for indirect identifiers in free text, including personal names, employer names, institution names, course names, project names and client names, then a person reviews the record before it enters any research or improvement set. We do not attempt to re-identify it, and our terms forbid customers from trying.

Session Research. Optional, and only with your explicit consent.

Some research needs the actual session: the prompts as written, the sequence of edits, the moments of verification. That material cannot be reliably stripped of everything that might identify its author, because writing itself is distinctive. We do not pretend otherwise, and that is precisely why we ask rather than treating it as anonymous. We use it only where the participant said yes. Published datasets contain only the sessions of participants who said yes.

If a record cannot be brought to the de-identified standard, it is not used for Aggregate Research either. It goes into the consented set or it goes nowhere.

One question, on its own screen, after the assessment notice and before the task begins. Neither answer is pre-selected. Skipping it is recorded as a refusal.

  • No pre-selected option and no default. A non-answer is a no.
  • Your answer is stored with a timestamp and the identifier of the exact wording you saw, whether you said yes, said no, or skipped it. We keep superseded wordings, so we can always show what you were asked.

Your instructor cannot find out, and our contract forbids a customer from asking or from telling you the answer matters.
  • Withdrawal is a real route with a named person responsible for it.
  • Consent is per session. Saying yes once does not commit you for any later assessment.
  • We never ask this question of anyone who has not confirmed they are 18 or over, and the platform is not open to anyone under 18.

Consent here is consent under article 6(1)(a) of the GDPR and the equivalent provisions of DIFC Data Protection Law. It is separate from anything the customer relies on, and refusing it has no effect on your assessment or your grade.

It is stored apart from your name and contact details. It is read by our researchers and by independent academic reviewers and collaborators who take part in scoring-agreement studies. Each of them is engaged under a written agreement imposing confidentiality, use restricted to the study, a ban on re-identification and on passing the data on, and a return or destruction obligation, with a lawful transfer mechanism in place. They never receive your contact details.

Your session may appear in a published paper, or in a published dataset, with identifying material removed first, subject to the limit described in section 5.2.

We keep it until you withdraw. There is no fixed end date, because published findings have to remain reproducible and a dataset that disappears cannot be checked.
